Songs for Games That Don't Exist

Every track on this album started with me letting a wheel decide everything: the setting, the lead instrument, the mood, the tempo, the time signature, the key, and one wildcard rule I had to follow. Then I had to make it work. The tags under each track are what the wheel gave me.

Underwater world

Beneath the Tides

  • Solo piano
  • Contemplative
  • Allegretto
  • E♭ major
  • 4/4
  • 9:4 polyrhythm

My favorite track on the album. The wheel gave me a 9 against 4 polyrhythm, so the two hands play at different speeds. They drift apart and only line back up at the end of each cycle, kind of like currents moving under the surface.

Magical academy

Candlelight Studies

  • Soprano saxophone
  • Contemplative
  • 63 BPM
  • 4/8
  • A minor
  • Planing harmony

I pictured a student alone in the library late at night. The chords all move in parallel (planing harmony), which gives the magic this floating feel, like it's not quite following the normal rules.

Dark fantasy

Sinister Waltz

  • Bowed guitar
  • Graceful

Elegant on the surface, with something ominous going on underneath.

Sci-fi space

T U R N B A C K

  • Theremin
  • Mercurial
  • Chain of suspensions

A warning from deep space. There's morse code hidden in the track, and every chord leans into the next one without ever really resolving (that's the chain of suspensions).

Ancient Egypt

Bastet's Dance

  • Bass clarinet
  • Romantic
  • 15/8
  • Octatonic scale

The wheel landed on 15/8 and an octatonic scale, so it's a dance that keeps you a little off balance and sounds ancient and strange at the same time.

Volcanic hellscape

Pressure

  • Cimbalom
  • Paranoid

Constant, rising tension. The ground's about to give way and the whole track knows it.

Ore

My second concept album. Twelve tracks ordered by depth, from emerald down to ancient debris, tied together by a pickaxe theme on xylophone. All twelve tracks are up on my Instagram, and the full album is coming to streaming soon.

Commissioned D&D campaign music

My first commission: seven tracks for a private D&D campaign. The first five are each a completely different village with its own sound, but they're all tied together by one leitmotif. Track six brings all five together into one town (kind of inspired by Tarrey Town), and track seven takes things up to the moon. It shows one way I can approach a project: one main theme that connects the whole world around it.
